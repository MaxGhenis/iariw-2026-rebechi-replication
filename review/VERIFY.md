## What the memo misses or gets backwards

1. **The authors already concede most of the central econometric limitations.** They say state trends move estimates “much closer to zero” (`paper.txt` 685–690), SDID group estimates are n.s. and the DDD SE is approximate (792–802), NC/ND/OH are the negative outliers (803–827), “most” results come from a few outlying states (1067–1070), political first-stage significance occurs only in corporate-only states and observed channels are incomplete (940–958), and corporate-only tax effects are n.s. (1011–1015). The memo's useful additions are quantification, the joint leave-three-out, and outside-source reconciliation; it should not present the underlying concessions as discoveries.

2. **The public reconstruction fails a headline dynamic result that the memo does not emphasize.** Independent Table-5-style tests through the paper's written +10 endpoint give both-ban post p `.460` for top-1 ATR and `.175` for RS, versus authors' `.025/.049`; including 2021 gives `.525/.225`. Cross-ban post differences remain highly significant. This should sit beside the already disclosed overall-ATR non-replication. Average TWFE stars reproducing does not imply that every dynamic inference reproduces.

3. **Appendix D contradicts its own prose and slightly strengthens the pretrend concern.** The text says all joint pretests fail to reject (`paper.txt` 2235–2239), but Table D-3 reports both-ban RS p `.085` (2340–2346), which rejects at 10%. Slide 12 is accurate for main Table 5 only.

4. **Income-tax-only SDID partly answers A4.** Main-sample SDID group effects are n.s., but Table B-2 restores both-ban top-1 significance at 10%, `-.57 (.30)`; RS remains n.s. The memo should report both facts.

5. **Table C-1 is not literally “digit for digit.”** Point estimates match Table 4, but the both-ban top-1 SE is `.25` rather than `.24`, and C-1 has no DDD row. Its point-estimate redundancy remains a valid criticism.

6. **A DDD between historically different blocs is not automatically the marginal causal effect of lifting a union ban.** That interpretation requires comparability/homogeneous corporate-ban effects. The paper's and memo's own state-type evidence makes that assumption doubtful.

7. **Figure 5 is state-specific SDID, not a decomposition of the TWFE coefficient.** Its ranking corroborates concentration, but the public per-state DiDs are a descriptive diagnostic. The paper already reports single-state LOO; the genuinely new stress test is dropping NC+ND+OH jointly.

8. **Several source claims need reversal or narrowing.** The paper does not cite Abdul-Razzak for the “opposite” historical-adoption claim; KMW does not formally test ban type versus redistricting; NCSL does not directly yield 13/8; opaque 501(c)/527 funding cannot be called noncorporate; and `$1.6m` is not per cycle. Those are memo errors, not author shortcomings.

9. **The economic-freedom critique is only half circular.** EFNA embeds closely related statutory tax and union inputs, but not the simulated ATR/RS outcome; Figure 7(b)'s regulation index is separate evidence and should be credited.

10. **“No income tax” is too loose.** Table B-1 drops eight jurisdictions, but NH and TN had limited interest/dividend taxes. In the both-ban group, AK/TX/WY are exact-zero PIT paths and NH is small, not identically zero. Use “no or limited broad wage income tax.”

11. **Benefits are undocumented, not proved zero.** `B` appears in the equations, while only TAXSIM tax/credit liabilities are described. Without authors' code, the safe challenge is to ask how `B` was populated. Similarly, the replication script uses subgroup state-year ATRs, not a literal household regression.

12. **The paper's “top income tax rates” label is imprecise.** Its headline outcomes are simulated average tax rates, which the methods explicitly distinguish from statutory marginal rates (`paper.txt` 398–403). For the fixed national top 1%, they closely track top-rate reforms, but they are not identical.

13. **The event window is internally incomplete.** Equation (8) specifies `j=-6,…,+10` although 2004–2021 relative to 2010 includes `+11`; the treatment indicator is described as one “after 2010” while 2010 is event time zero. The memo catches general timing ambiguity but misses this exact endpoint.

14. **Income-tax RS is not full fiscal redistribution.** Several driver reforms also changed sales-tax bases/rates or refundable credits. With benefits and non-income taxes outside the documented simulation, neither paper nor memo can infer the net state-fiscal incidence from the income-tax RS alone.

15. **Some claims are inherently time- or corpus-dependent.** “No package exists anywhere” must be dated to the named searches; “most research” has an undefined denominator; D5 depends on a figure read and conflicts with the public panel. These should not be categorical room statements.

## Overall judgment

The memo is directionally fair and empirically valuable—the public reconstruction supports the headline average effects while sharpening serious identification, interpretation, concentration, and documentation concerns—but it is not yet fair enough to present verbatim because it under-credits the authors' explicit concessions and occasionally converts suggestive comparisons into causal or source claims. The three most defensible findings in the room are **B1** (abstract omits the both-ban conditionality and no pooled row is reported), **A1** (the gap was already widening and trends materially attenuate the estimate), and **A2/A3** (five-state concentration, reform timing, and the decisive joint leave-three-out). The three most fragile are **B4** (refuted as written), **D5** (unrecoverable figure read and opposite public path), and **C3** (an undefined “most of the literature” count). Verdict counts across the 37 audited Bottom-line/§2/§3 entries are **13 CONFIRMED, 22 PLAUSIBLE, and 2 REFUTED**.
